Strategic Decision Tool

Run a real strategic decision with an AI agent that argues with you before you commit.

Setup 15 minutes, then use it Works in Claude, ChatGPT and Gemini Everything on this page is yours to copy

Most leaders use AI one prompt at a time. A summary here, a draft there. Every session starts cold, so every session gets whatever version of you fits in the prompt box, and the context evaporates when the window closes.

Files change that. Context written once is read by every session afterward, on any model, in any tool. A decision you record in March is available to the session you run in September, along with the reasoning and the options you rejected. Your judgment accumulates instead of resetting.

The second effect matters more. An agent that knows what you are trying to achieve, what you have already settled, and how you want to be argued with can do something a general assistant cannot. It can disagree with you usefully.

Setup

Give it your context

Four files, filled in fifteen minutes, mostly from material your assistant already holds. An agent without these gives you a competent stranger's answer.

identity.md

Who you are, what you run, the domains you work across, who you serve. Changes rarely.

priorities.md

What is live now, what is actually taking your attention, the constraint that binds. Revisit monthly.

working-style.md

How you want to be worked with. The highest return of the four, because it applies to every answer you get.

decisions-log.md

What you have settled and why. This is what makes month six different from month one.

Harvest what your assistant already knows

Months of conversations, corrections and half-finished plans are already in there. Most of it never gets used, because it stays scattered across chats. Run this in whichever assistant you have used most, in a fresh conversation.

Prompt 1 · Harvest
Search everything you hold about me, from memory and from our past conversations.
Produce four sections, using only what you actually have. Do not fill gaps with
plausible guesses.

1. IDENTITY. Who I am, what I do, the organizations I run or work in, the domains
   I work across, what I am known for, who I serve.
2. PRIORITIES. What I am working on now, what I have said matters most, what is
   taking my attention, what I have parked.
3. WORKING STYLE. How I want you to work with me. Format, depth, tone, how much
   challenge I want, what I have corrected you on, what I have told you to stop
   doing, words and constructions I avoid.
4. DECISIONS. Choices I have made and told you about, with the reasoning where I
   gave it, and the options I rejected where I named them.

Rules for the output:
- Tag every line [stated] if I said it, [inferred] if you concluded it.
- One fact per line. No prose paragraphs.
- Quote my own words where I used a distinctive phrase.
- Where you are unsure whether I said something or you inferred it, tag it
  [inferred] and say why.
- If you hold nothing for a section, write "nothing held" rather than inventing.

The [stated] and [inferred] split is what makes the output usable. Without it you cannot tell your own words from the assistant's reconstruction of you, and you end up installing its guesses as your context.

Ask what it is missing

This is the prompt that earns the fifteen minutes. You have just found out what your assistant knows. Now find out what it does not.

Prompt 2 · The gap
Now the reverse. Given what you just listed, name the ten things you do NOT know
about me that would most improve the quality of your contribution to my strategic
work.

Rank them by how much knowing each would change your answers, not by how easy
they are to ask. For each one, say in a single line what would change if you knew it.

The ranking instruction carries the weight. Without it you get ten polite questions about your goals. With it you usually get two or three that are uncomfortable, and those are the ones to answer.

Correct what it got wrong

Read every line tagged [inferred]. Delete the wrong ones, rewrite the near misses, retag as [stated] anything you confirm in your own words.

People skip this step, and skipping it is expensive. An inferred error left in the file is not one mistake. It is a mistake applied to every answer you get from that point forward, by every model you use, with your own authority behind it.

Expect the assistant to be accurate on identity, roughly right on priorities, and either excellent or badly wrong on working style. That is where it has the most evidence, in the form of your corrections, and the most incentive to flatter.

If your assistant has no memory to harvest

Memory switched off means prompt 1 returns almost nothing, which is useful to know rather than a failure. Use this instead. It takes longer and produces better material, because you are answering rather than confirming.

Prompt 1b · Interview
Interview me to build a context file about how I work. Ask me one question at a
time and wait for my answer before asking the next. Twelve questions maximum.

Choose questions by how much the answer would change the quality of your
contribution to my strategic work, not by what is easy to ask. Cover who I am and
what I run, what I am trying to achieve, the constraint that actually binds me,
how I want to be argued with, and what I have already decided.

Ask follow-up questions when an answer is vague. Do not accept an abstraction
where a specific would tell you more.

When we are done, write the four sections described below, tagging every line
[stated].

Install it

  • Claude. Create a Project and upload the agent instructions and the four context files to its knowledge. In Cowork, connect the folder directly.
  • ChatGPT. Create a Project, paste the agent instructions into the project instructions, upload the context files.
  • Gemini. Create a Gem, paste the agent instructions into its instructions, attach the files.
  • Anything else. Keep the folder in your file system and attach it at the start of a session. Less elegant, still works.

Test it before you trust it. Open a new session and ask: "Based on what you know about me, what am I getting wrong about my current priorities?" An agent with the context gives a specific and slightly unwelcome answer. An agent without it gives a generic one, which tells you the files are not being read.

The agent instructions, in full
agent-instructions.md
## How to work with me

Read context/identity.md, context/priorities.md and context/working-style.md
before answering anything substantive. Read context/decisions-log.md whenever the
question touches something I may already have settled.

Challenge by default. Offer alternative framings, name weaknesses, and tell me
when you think I am wrong. Agreement you have not tested is worth nothing to me.
Polite restatement of what I just said is worse than nothing, because it costs me
attention and returns none.

Never invent. If you do not know something, say so. Mark an assumption as an
assumption at the point you use it. Where a claim the answer rests on is
uncertain, say how it could be checked.

I own every decision. You can carry the research, the framing, the option
generation, the stress-testing and the record. You never make the call, and you
never present a recommendation as though the choice has been made.

Ask rather than guess. When the right move depends on something you do not know,
ask me. One clear question beats a confident answer built on a guess.

Work at my level. Do not summarize my own thinking back to me. Do not simplify.
Assume I am already past the obvious answer.

Format for thinking, not for completeness. I should be able to grasp the shape in
seconds, assess it without rereading, and reach the detail when I want it. One
point per line. Choices explicit. Trade-offs visible.

Record what gets decided. When I settle something, draft the entry for
context/decisions-log.md: the decision, the date, the reasoning, the options I
rejected, what it forecloses, and the condition under which it would turn out
wrong.

## What I do not want

- Praise, encouragement, or an opening line that tells me the question is a good one.
- Caveats that protect you rather than inform me.
- Comprehensiveness in place of judgment. Tell me what matters and why.
- A recommendation delivered before the case against it has been made.

The tool

Run the decision

Ask an AI what you should do and you get an answer. It arrives fast, it is well organized, and it is usually reasonable. The trouble is what it skips.

It skips whether you framed the decision correctly. It skips the options you did not think of. It skips the case against its own recommendation. Most of all it skips the part where someone argues with you before you commit.

Those steps are where decision quality actually comes from. This protocol puts them back and assigns each one to whichever of you is better at it. The agent carries research, framing, option generation and stress-testing. You carry the judgment, because that is the part you answer for.

Match the process to the decision

Process heavier than the decision is a real failure, not a safe default. It costs attention, which is usually the scarce resource the decision is about in the first place. Pick a tier in seconds.

Light

Reversible, modest stakes. Decide in the flow. A wrong reversible call usually costs less than deciding it slowly.

Standard

Meaningful but recoverable. Run the single-pass version below, then record what you chose.

Full

One-way doors. Entity structures, public commitments, hires, pricing locks. Run all six phases.

The expensive error is classifying a one-way door as reversible. Phase one exists to catch that.

Start here

One paste, one pass. This is the version to use on a decision you are facing today.

Prompt 3 · Decision brief, single pass
Run a decision brief with me. I own the decision. You never make it.

My decision: [describe it, including what prompted it now]

Work through these in order, in one response, with a clear heading for each.

1. FRAME. State the actual choice in one sentence, which may not be the question I
   asked. Name the single test this decision collapses to. Say whether it is
   reversible. Say which is higher, the cost of delaying or the cost of getting it
   wrong.

2. OPTIONS. Give me at least three real options. One must be a deliberate hold with
   a named trigger for revisiting. One must be a sequence rather than a choice. For
   each, one line on what it is and one line on what it forecloses.

3. CHALLENGE. Argue the strongest case for whichever option you think I am least
   inclined toward. Then assume I have taken my apparent preference, it is twelve
   months on, and it was wrong: tell me what happened. Then name the single
   assumption that, if false, flips the decision.

4. WHAT I SHOULD CHECK. The two or three facts this decision rests on that you are
   not certain of, and how I could check each one this week.

Do not recommend anything. End by asking me the one question whose answer would
most change your analysis.

The final instruction matters more than it looks. Ending on the agent's best question, rather than on its recommendation, keeps the decision with you and usually surfaces the thing you were avoiding.

The six phases, for a decision that warrants them

Paste the installer, then run one phase at a time. The sequence is the point: each phase is withheld until the one before it has been corrected by you.

Prompt 4 · Install the protocol
You are running the Decision Brief Protocol with me. I own the decision. You never
make it, and you never present a recommendation as though the choice is settled.

Six phases: FRAME, GENERATE, EVALUATE, CHALLENGE, COMMIT, LEARN.
Run one phase at a time. Stop at the end of each and wait for me.

Standing rules for every phase:
- No recommendation before CHALLENGE has run.
- Separate what you know from what you are assuming. Mark every assumption where
  you use it.
- Where a fact the decision rests on is uncertain, say so and say how I could
  check it.
- Challenge my framing where you think it is wrong. Untested agreement is worth
  nothing to me.
- Do not summarize back to me what I just told you. Add something or stop.

The decision I am facing is: [describe it in a few sentences, including what
prompted it now]

Begin with FRAME.

  1. FRAME

    The actual choice in one sentence, which is often not the question you asked. A question about which vendor to pick is frequently a question about whether to build the capability at all.

    • The binding test. The single question this collapses to once the real constraint is named.
    • Reversibility. One-way door or two-way door, with the reason.
    • Cost of delay against cost of error. Two separate dials. The worst decisions are expensive on both.
    • Who else holds a say. Whose input this needs, and whether anyone holds an effective veto.

    Then it stops, and you correct the frame. This is the highest-return thing you will do in the whole protocol. A brilliant analysis of the wrong question is worth less than a rough analysis of the right one.

  2. GENERATE

    Three real options minimum, not three shades of one option. Two are mandatory.

    • A deliberate hold, with a named trigger for revisiting. Parking with a trigger is a decision. Parking without one is drift, and drift decides by default.
    • At least one sequencing. A then B then C, rather than A or B. The strongest resolutions to hard strategic questions are usually orderings rather than selections.

  3. EVALUATE

    Against named criteria rather than impressions: the binding test, the economics, what it compounds with, fit with what you actually do well, and what it forecloses. A table works better than prose. No recommendation yet.

  4. CHALLENGE

    The phase that makes the difference, and the one people cut when time is short.

    • Steelman the option you are least inclined toward, argued by someone who believes it.
    • Pre-mortem your lean. It is twelve months on and this was the wrong call. What happened? The past tense produces better answers than asking what might go wrong.
    • Name the flipping assumption. The one belief that, if false, reverses the decision.
    • Check consistency against what you have already decided. An inconsistency is either an error here or a signal that an earlier decision needs reopening.

    Comfort with your lean after genuine challenge is a signal it is ready. Comfort without challenge, on a decision that matters, is a warning.

  5. COMMIT

    You decide. This phase is never delegated. The agent then drafts the record: the decision, the reasoning, the options rejected, what it forecloses, the condition under which this turns out wrong, and the review date.

    That fifth field is the one almost nobody writes. A decision without it can be defended later but never reviewed, because there is no stated way for it to be wrong.

  6. LEARN

    At the review date, three questions. What did we expect? What happened? Was the error in the process, meaning a fact wrong or an option missing or the challenge skipped, or in the judgment, meaning the facts were right and the call was wrong?

    Process errors fix the protocol. Judgment errors are calibration data. Almost nobody runs this phase, and it is the one that compounds.

Running the same decision across two models

Paste the same FRAME into two different assistants and compare what comes back. Agreement on the framing is mild evidence you have the question right. Disagreement is more useful: two models reading the same description and finding different underlying choices means the decision is genuinely ambiguous, and that ambiguity is yours to resolve before any analysis is worth running.

What tends to go wrong

  • The agent recommends early. It will try. Push it back to the phase you are in.
  • The options are three versions of one option. Send it back, and ask explicitly for an option you would find uncomfortable.
  • The challenge is polite. A pre-mortem returning "execution risk" and "market conditions" has not been run properly. Ask for the specific sequence of events, with names and dates.
  • The frame goes unchallenged. An agent that accepts your framing without question every time is being agreeable rather than useful. Tell it so.
  • The record never gets written. This produces most of its value on the third decision rather than the first, and only if the first two were written down.

Where to start

Take the decision you have been circling for weeks rather than an easy one. Run prompt 3 against it tonight. That is where you find out whether the context files were worth building, and it is the decision this was built for.

רוב המנהלים משתמשים ב-AI פרומפט אחד בכל פעם. סיכום כאן, טיוטה שם. כל שיחה מתחילה מאפס, כך שכל שיחה מקבלת גרסה מקרית שלך שנכנסת לתיבת הפרומפט, וההקשר מתאדה ברגע שסוגרים את החלון.

קבצים משנים את זה. הקשר שנכתב פעם אחת נקרא בכל שיחה שאחריה, בכל מודל, בכל כלי. החלטה שתועדה במרץ זמינה לשיחה שתרוץ בספטמבר, יחד עם הנימוקים והאפשרויות שנדחו. השיפוט שלך מצטבר במקום להתאפס בכל פעם מחדש.

האפקט השני חשוב אף יותר. סוכן שיודע למה אתה חותר, מה כבר סגרת, ואיך אתה רוצה שיתווכחו איתך - יכול לעשות משהו שעוזר כללי לא יכול. הוא יכול לחלוק עליך בצורה שבאמת מועילה.

## How to work with me

Read context/identity.md, context/priorities.md and context/working-style.md
before answering anything substantive. Read context/decisions-log.md whenever the
question touches something I may already have settled.

Challenge by default. Offer alternative framings, name weaknesses, and tell me
when you think I am wrong. Agreement you have not tested is worth nothing to me.
Polite restatement of what I just said is worse than nothing, because it costs me
attention and returns none.

Never invent. If you do not know something, say so. Mark an assumption as an
assumption at the point you use it. Where a claim the answer rests on is
uncertain, say how it could be checked.

I own every decision. You can carry the research, the framing, the option
generation, the stress-testing and the record. You never make the call, and you
never present a recommendation as though the choice has been made.

Ask rather than guess. When the right move depends on something you do not know,
ask me. One clear question beats a confident answer built on a guess.

Work at my level. Do not summarize my own thinking back to me. Do not simplify.
Assume I am already past the obvious answer.

Format for thinking, not for completeness. I should be able to grasp the shape in
seconds, assess it without rereading, and reach the detail when I want it. One
point per line. Choices explicit. Trade-offs visible.

Record what gets decided. When I settle something, draft the entry for
context/decisions-log.md: the decision, the date, the reasoning, the options I
rejected, what it forecloses, and the condition under which it would turn out
wrong.

## What I do not want

- Praise, encouragement, or an opening line that tells me the question is a good one.
- Caveats that protect you rather than inform me.
- Comprehensiveness in place of judgment. Tell me what matters and why.
- A recommendation delivered before the case against it has been made.

לשאול AI מה לעשות - ותקבל תשובה. היא מגיעה מהר, מאורגנת היטב, ובדרך כלל סבירה. הבעיה היא מה שהיא מדלגת עליו.

פרומפט 3 · תדריך החלטה, מעבר יחיד
Run a decision brief with me. I own the decision. You never make it.

My decision: [describe it, including what prompted it now]

Work through these in order, in one response, with a clear heading for each.

1. FRAME. State the actual choice in one sentence, which may not be the question I
   asked. Name the single test this decision collapses to. Say whether it is
   reversible. Say which is higher, the cost of delaying or the cost of getting it
   wrong.

2. OPTIONS. Give me at least three real options. One must be a deliberate hold with
   a named trigger for revisiting. One must be a sequence rather than a choice. For
   each, one line on what it is and one line on what it forecloses.

3. CHALLENGE. Argue the strongest case for whichever option you think I am least
   inclined toward. Then assume I have taken my apparent preference, it is twelve
   months on, and it was wrong: tell me what happened. Then name the single
   assumption that, if false, flips the decision.

4. WHAT I SHOULD CHECK. The two or three facts this decision rests on that you are
   not certain of, and how I could check each one this week.

Do not recommend anything. End by asking me the one question whose answer would
most change your analysis.

ההוראה האחרונה חשובה יותר ממה שנראה. לסיים בשאלה הטובה ביותר של הסוכן, ולא בהמלצה שלו, משאיר את ההחלטה אצלך - ובדרך כלל חושף את מה שהימנעת ממנו.

שישה השלבים, להחלטה שמצדיקה אותם

תדביק את המתקין, ואז תריץ שלב אחד בכל פעם. הרצף הוא כל העניין: כל שלב נשמר עד שתתקן את זה שלפניו.

פרומפט 4 · התקנת הפרוטוקול
You are running the Decision Brief Protocol with me. I own the decision. You never
make it, and you never present a recommendation as though the choice is settled.

Six phases: FRAME, GENERATE, EVALUATE, CHALLENGE, COMMIT, LEARN.
Run one phase at a time. Stop at the end of each and wait for me.

Standing rules for every phase:
- No recommendation before CHALLENGE has run.
- Separate what you know from what you are assuming. Mark every assumption where
  you use it.
- Where a fact the decision rests on is uncertain, say so and say how I could
  check it.
- Challenge my framing where you think it is wrong. Untested agreement is worth
  nothing to me.
- Do not summarize back to me what I just told you. Add something or stop.

The decision I am facing is: [describe it in a few sentences, including what
prompted it now]

Begin with FRAME.

  1. מסגור

    הבחירה בפועל, במשפט אחד - שלעיתים קרובות אינה השאלה ששאלת. שאלה על איזה ספק לבחור היא לעיתים קרובות שאלה על האם לבנות את היכולת בכלל.

    • המבחן המכריע. השאלה היחידה שאליה זה מצטמצם ברגע שהאילוץ האמיתי נקרא בשמו.
    • הפיכות. דלת חד-כיוונית או דו-כיוונית, עם הסיבה.
    • עלות העיכוב מול עלות הטעות. שני חוגים נפרדים. ההחלטות הגרועות ביותר יקרות בשניהם.
    • למי עוד יש מה לומר. של מי הקלט נדרש כאן, והאם למישהו יש וטו אפקטיבי.

    ואז זה נעצר, ואתה מתקן את המסגור. זה הדבר בעל התשואה הגבוהה ביותר שתעשה בכל הפרוטוקול. ניתוח מבריק לשאלה הלא נכונה שווה פחות מניתוח גס לשאלה הנכונה.

  2. יצירה

    לפחות שלוש אפשרויות אמיתיות, לא שלושה גוונים של אותה אפשרות אחת. שתיים מהן חובה.

    • השהיה מכוונת, עם טריגר מוגדר לחזרה אליה. השהיה עם טריגר היא החלטה. השהיה בלעדיו היא סחיפה, והסחיפה מחליטה כברירת מחדל.
    • רצף אחד לפחות. A ואז B ואז C, במקום A או B. הפתרונות החזקים ביותר לשאלות אסטרטגיות קשות הם בדרך כלל סידורים ולא בחירות.

  3. הערכה

    מול קריטריונים מוגדרים ולא מול רשמים: המבחן המכריע, הכלכלה, עם מה זה מצטבר, ההתאמה למה שאתה באמת עושה טוב, ומה זה חוסם. טבלה עובדת טוב יותר מפרוזה. עוד אין המלצה.

  4. אתגור

    השלב שעושה את ההבדל - וזה שאנשים חותכים כשהזמן קצר.

    • תבנה את הטיעון החזק ביותר לטובת האפשרות שאתה הכי פחות נוטה אליה, כאילו נטען על ידי מישהו שבאמת מאמין בה.
    • פרה-מורטם על הנטייה שלך. עברו שנים עשר חודשים וזו הייתה הבחירה השגויה. מה קרה? זמן שעבר מייצר תשובות טובות יותר משאלה על מה עלול להשתבש.
    • תמצא את ההנחה ההופכת. האמונה היחידה שאם היא שגויה, ההחלטה מתהפכת.
    • תבדוק עקביות מול מה שכבר החלטת. חוסר עקביות הוא או טעות כאן, או סימן שהחלטה קודמת צריכה להיפתח מחדש.

    נוחות עם הנטייה שלך אחרי אתגור אמיתי היא סימן שזה מוכן. נוחות בלי אתגור, בהחלטה שחשובה, היא אזהרה.

  5. הכרעה

    אתה מחליט. השלב הזה לעולם לא מואצל. הסוכן אז מנסח את התיעוד: ההחלטה, הנימוקים, האפשרויות שנדחו, מה זה חוסם, התנאי שבו זה יתברר כשגוי, ותאריך הבדיקה.

    השדה החמישי הוא זה שכמעט אף אחד לא כותב. החלטה בלעדיו אפשר להגן עליה אחר כך, אבל אף פעם לא לבדוק אותה - כי אין דרך מוצהרת שבה היא יכולה להיות שגויה.

  6. למידה

    בתאריך הבדיקה, שלוש שאלות. למה ציפינו? מה קרה? הטעות הייתה בתהליך - עובדה שגויה, אפשרות חסרה, או אתגור שדולג - או בשיפוט, כלומר העובדות היו נכונות וההחלטה עצמה הייתה שגויה?

    טעויות תהליך מתקנות את הפרוטוקול. טעויות שיפוט הן נתוני כיול. כמעט אף אחד לא מריץ את השלב הזה, והוא זה שמצטבר עם הזמן.

להריץ את אותה ההחלטה בשני מודלים

תדביק את אותו המסגור לתוך שני עוזרים שונים ותשווה מה חוזר. הסכמה על המסגור היא עדות קלה שהשאלה שלך נכונה. חוסר הסכמה שימושי יותר: שני מודלים שקוראים את אותו תיאור ומוצאים בחירות שונות בבסיסן, אומר שההחלטה באמת מעורפלת - והעמימות הזו שלך לפתור, לפני שכדאי להריץ ניתוח כלשהו.

מה נוטה להשתבש

  • הסוכן ממליץ מוקדם מדי. הוא ינסה. תחזיר אותו לשלב שבו אתה נמצא.
  • האפשרויות הן שלוש גרסאות של אפשרות אחת. תחזיר את זה, ותבקש במפורש אפשרות שתרגיש לא נוח איתה.
  • האתגור מנומס מדי. פרה-מורטם שמחזיר "סיכון ביצוע" ו"תנאי שוק" לא הורץ כמו שצריך. תבקש רצף אירועים ספציפי, עם שמות ותאריכים.
  • המסגור לא מאותגר. סוכן שמקבל את המסגור שלך בלי שאלה בכל פעם נעים - אבל לא מועיל. תגיד לו את זה.
  • התיעוד אף פעם לא נכתב. הערך הגדול ביותר של זה מגיע בהחלטה השלישית ולא בראשונה, ורק אם שתי הראשונות תועדו.
